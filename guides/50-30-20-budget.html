The 50/30/20 Budget Rule: A Simple Budget That Actually Sticks

How the 50/30/20 budget works, with examples, when to adjust it, and a free calculator.

SCBy the Snap.Cash Editorial Team · Updated Sep 30, 2026 · 6 min read

The 50/30/20 rule splits your after-tax income into three buckets: 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% savings and extra debt payments. It's popular because it's simple enough to follow without tracking every coffee.

What counts as a need?

Rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments, transportation to work and basic childcare.

What counts as a want?

Dining out, streaming, travel, hobbies, upgrades beyond the basics.

The 20%

Emergency fund first (aim for 3–6 months of essentials), then high-interest debt, then retirement and investing.

When to adjust

In high-cost cities, needs often exceed 50%. Try 60/20/20 and use side income or cashback to close the gap. If you have high-interest debt, a temporary 50/20/30 (30% to debt) pays it off far faster — try the debt payoff calculator.

Side Hustle Taxes Explained: What to Set Aside and What You Can Deduct

How side hustle income is taxed, how much to set aside, common deductions and record-keeping tips (general education, not tax advice).

SCBy the Snap.Cash Editorial Team · Updated Sep 30, 2026 · 7 min read

Side income is taxable in almost every country — even if you don't receive a tax form. The good news: you can often deduct legitimate business expenses.

This guide is general education, not tax advice. Rules vary by country and state/province — confirm with a qualified tax professional.

How much to set aside

A common rule of thumb is 20–30% of net side income. In the U.S., self-employed people also pay self-employment tax (Social Security and Medicare) on net earnings, and may need to make quarterly estimated payments.

Common deductible expenses

  • Business mileage or actual vehicle costs (gig drivers).
  • A portion of phone and internet used for the work.
  • Equipment, software and supplies.
  • Platform and payment-processing fees.
  • Home office (if it meets the rules).

Keep clean records

  • Use a separate bank account for side income.
  • Track mileage with an app from day one.
  • Save receipts digitally.
